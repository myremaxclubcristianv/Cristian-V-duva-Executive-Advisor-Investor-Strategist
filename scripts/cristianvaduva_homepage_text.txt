The house, built in an eclectic style typical of its era, features a central body (C1) with a basement, ground floor, first floor, and attic (where the owner lived). It also includes a servants' wing and an annex building (C2) with a basement, ground floor, and mezzanine. The annex was used as a stable for four horses and a carriage shed, accessible via a spiral staircase in a slender pointed tower, characteristic of 19th-century boyar houses in Bucharest.

While the architect of the central house body (C1) remains unknown, the architect of the servants' wing (C2), featuring the stable with the pointed tower, is Grigore Cerkez. He designed this section in 1883. Cerkez is renowned for several significant architectural projects in Bucharest and beyond, including a wing of Cotroceni Palace (Great Reception Hall, 1925), the Ion Mincu Institute of Architecture (1912-1927), the Odeon Theater (1911), Cantacuzino Castle in Bușteni (1911), Dissescu House on Calea Victoriei (1912), and the facade of the "Grigore Antipa" Museum (1904-1908).

The property is officially part of the Historic Center of the Capital, classified as a Grade B architectural monument, and is also included in the Hristo Botev architectural ensemble. It boasts a comprehensive renovation and restoration project prepared by architect Petre Vencu, a professor at the Ion Mincu Institute of Architecture, along with two technical assessments.

It is important to mention that there is also a project for the complete reconstruction of the property, along with the necessary renovation permits, which have approximately 250 days of validity remaining. These will be made available to the buyer.
